Analysis#

class SupportAnalyzer#
#include <support_analyzer.h>

Computes the inkjet support volume beneath an OpenVCAD design.

Inkjet printers cannot print a voxel unless there is already material in the column directly below it. This analyzer samples the input model on a uniform voxel grid and marks every empty voxel that has occupied model or support material above it, filling downward to the bottom of the model’s bounding box. XY columns are processed concurrently.

analyze() returns that occupancy as an implicit OpenVCAD solid (a signed-distance field). Downstream code can fill the solid with support material, a lattice, or any other OpenVCAD geometry. The input model is not modified.

Functions

MassPropertiesReport mass_properties(const std::shared_ptr<Node> &root, double voxel_size, const MassPropertiesOptions &options = MassPropertiesOptions())#

Computes volume, surface area, mass, centre of mass and inertia of a node.

The node is sampled at voxel centres on a uniform grid over its bounding box, one voxel wider on every side and shifted by a fixed fraction of a voxel so that flat faces bounding the box do not fall on voxel boundaries. Away from the surface a voxel is wholly inside or outside. Near the surface the signed distance d and its central-difference gradient g on the grid give a local plane; the voxel is weighted by the exact fraction of the cube on its inside, with the exact first and second moments of that part along the normal, and the plane’s area inside the voxel adds to the surface area. Dividing by |g| makes this hold for fields that are not true distances (TPMS, scaled bodies).

Accuracy. On smooth surfaces the error falls with the square of the voxel size h: a sphere of radius R reads about (h/R)^2 / 4 high in volume. Sharp edges and corners are rounded off by about a voxel, so on a sharp-edged part volume reads slightly high and surface area slightly low, by amounts that fall linearly with h (a box 16 voxels thick: about 0.2% and 3%). With estimate_error on, the integration is repeated at 2h and each error is estimated as |Q_h - Q_2h|: the error itself where it falls linearly, three times it where it falls with the square, so the estimate is conservative once every feature spans several voxels. Features thinner than about two voxels are not resolved.

Density. Mass, centre of mass and inertia need a density in g/cm^3. It comes from one of three sources:

  1. a density attribute modelled on the design (options.density_attribute, default “density”);

  2. volume fractions times each material’s density from options.materials (MaterialDefs::density(), the optional “density” key of a material configuration). Fractions are normalised to sum to one, and the void material has density 0;

  3. a uniform density (options.density), for a design that is only geometry.

With MassSource::automatic (the default) the first source the design offers is used, point by point in that order: a point without the density attribute uses its volume fractions, then the uniform density. A design that offers several sources can name one with options.mass_source; options.density then fills in wherever that source is absent. When the design offers no source at all, mass, centre of mass and inertia are left empty and the report still gives volume, area and centroid.

The call never guesses a density. It throws when the chosen source is missing from the design, when a material present in the volume fractions has no density, or when part of the volume has no density from any available source.

Attributes are sampled once per voxel: at its centre, or just inside the surface along the grid normal for a cut voxel whose centre is outside. A boundary between materials or attribute values inside the part is therefore placed to within half a voxel. Voxels where the field kinks (sharp edges, creases between bodies, curvature radius under about seven voxels) are split into eight half-size pieces for the geometry. Z-slabs are sampled in parallel with thread-local clones; sums are taken per row and added in order, so results do not depend on threading.

Parameters:

  • root – Node to analyse. It is prepared with the voxel size unless options say it already is.

  • voxel_size – Positive sampling pitch in mm.

  • options – Density source, attributes to integrate, bounds and error estimate.

Throws:

std::runtime_error – for invalid input or missing densities, as described above.

Returns:

The report.

namespace PartialVolume#

Exact volume, area and moments of a unit cube cut by a plane, for partial-voxel weighting.

Analyses that integrate over a voxel grid (mass_properties()) treat the surface inside a voxel as the plane given by the signed distance and its gradient at the voxel centre. These helpers return the exact fraction of the voxel on the inside of that plane, the area of the plane inside the voxel and the first two moments of the inside part along the normal, so a planar surface is integrated exactly at any orientation.

Functions

inline CubeCut cut_unit_cube(const glm::dvec3 &normal, double offset)#

Cuts the unit cube [-1/2, 1/2]^3 with the half-space { x : normal .

x <= offset }.

Reflecting the axes makes the coefficients a, b, c non-negative; with s = a.y on the cube [0, 1]^3 and t = offset + (a + b + c) / 2, the inside part is s <= t. Its volume is the inclusion-exclusion sum G_3(t), where G_j(t) = sum over the corners e of (-1)^|e| max(0, t - a.e)^j / (j! abc). The area is G_2(t) (dV/dt for a unit normal), and the moments of s are t G_3 - G_4 and t^2 G_3 - 2 t G_4 + 2 G_5. Coefficients below 1e-6 are treated as zero and the 2D or 1D form is used, which avoids the cancellation of the 3D form.

An axis-aligned plane that lies on a cube face belongs to both neighbouring voxels. Its area is given to the cube below it (plane within 1e-5 of the top face, inclusive) and not to the cube above, so a face that falls on the voxel grid is counted exactly once.

Parameters:

  • normal – Unit normal of the plane, pointing out of the half-space.

  • offset – Signed offset of the plane from the cube centre along the normal, in side lengths.

Returns:

The inside fraction, the plane area inside the cube and the moments along the normal.

LocalThicknessReport local_thickness(const std::shared_ptr<Node> &root, double voxel_size, const LocalThicknessOptions &options = LocalThicknessOptions())#

Measures wall thickness (or pore size) at every point of a design, on a voxel grid.

The local thickness at a point is the diameter of the largest ball that fits inside the measured space and contains the point (the Hildebrand-Rueegsegger definition). In a plate it is the plate’s thickness, in a strut its diameter, and at a thin neck the neck’s width. Measuring the empty space instead gives pore size: the largest ball of empty space through each point, so channels too narrow to clear powder or resin read small.

Steps: the signed distance is sampled on a dense grid (bounds plus one voxel for the solid). The surface is sampled once per voxel of the other space that touches the measured space: the voxel centre moved along the grid normal by d / |g|. An exact separable Euclidean feature transform gives every voxel its nearest such sample, which answers “nearest surface point to here” in constant time. From each surface sample p a shrinking ball finds the largest ball tangent at p with no surface point inside: it starts large and shrinks to the ball through p and the intruding point until nothing intrudes, in a few steps. Every measured point lies in the ball of its nearest surface point, so the thickness at x is 2 max r over the balls that contain x; they are painted plane by plane in parallel in decreasing r so that each voxel is written once. Because distances are rebuilt from the sampled surface, the result does not depend on the field being a true distance inside: unions, lattices and TPMS fields (whose inside values are only bounds, or not distances at all) measure correctly.

Edges and walls. By definition no large ball reaches into a sharp convex edge or corner, so the field reads small in a band along them (about a fifth of the wall thickness wide at a 90-degree edge). That is not a thin wall. A ball whose shrinking stopped at a surface point with an opposing normal (more than 120 degrees apart) spans a wall, strut, neck or gap; the report’s minimum and regions count only places painted by such balls, which includes knife edges sharper than about 60 degrees, since those are thin.

Resolution limits. Thicknesses are accurate to about a tenth of a voxel for walls, struts and gaps two or more voxels thick. Thinner ones are unreliable and may vanish; use a voxel of at most a quarter of the smallest thickness that matters. Values at the cap mean “at least the cap”. For the empty space, pores within about one pore of the analysis box edge read as lower bounds, because balls are not searched outside the box. With bounds on the solid, walls cut by the box are measured only inside it.

The dense working arrays need about 17 bytes per voxel; the call throws before allocating more than max_memory_mb. The result is a sparse FloatVDBVolume holding values only in and next to the measured space.

Parameters:

  • root – Node to analyse. It is prepared with the voxel size unless options say it already is.

  • voxel_size – Positive sampling pitch in mm.

  • options – Phase, cap, threshold, histogram, bounds and memory limit.

Throws:

std::runtime_error – for invalid input or when the grid would exceed the memory limit.

Returns:

The report, whose attribute can be set on the analysed node to colour or grade by thickness.

namespace DistanceTransform#

Exact Euclidean feature transform on a dense voxel grid.

Used by local_thickness() to find, for every voxel, the nearest voxel of the other space, from which the inscribed radius is measured to a sub-voxel surface point.

Functions

void feature_transform(std::vector<uint32_t> &features, const glm::ivec3 &dimensions)#

Replaces every entry with the linear index of its nearest seed voxel (exact Euclidean).

Before the call, a seed voxel holds its own linear index and every other voxel holds no_feature. Indices are x-fastest: index = x + nx * (y + ny * z). After the call each voxel holds the index of a seed at the smallest Euclidean distance between voxel centres (ties go to the lower coordinate along the last axis processed). The transform is separable: a scan along X, then the lower envelope of parabolas along Y and along Z (Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher), each pass parallel over lines. Time is linear in the number of voxels.

Parameters:

  • features – The seed marks, replaced by nearest-seed indices; size nx * ny * nz.

  • dimensions – Grid size (nx, ny, nz), all positive, with nx * ny * nz below no_feature.

Throws:

std::runtime_error – when the size does not match the dimensions.

ConnectedComponentReport analyze_connected_components(const std::shared_ptr<Node> &root, const glm::vec3 &voxel_size, int connectivity = 26, const std::optional<std::pair<glm::vec3, glm::vec3>> &bounds = std::nullopt, bool root_already_prepared = false, bool cull_far_cells = false)#

Count the connected pieces of solid material in a node.

The node is sampled at voxel centres on a uniform grid covering its bounding box (or the supplied bounds). A sample is solid when the signed distance is <= 0. Solid samples are joined through 6- or 26-neighbourhoods with a streaming run-length union-find, so memory grows with the number of solid runs rather than the grid size. Z-slabs are sampled in parallel with thread-local clones of the node.

The result depends on resolution: necks thinner than about one voxel can be reported as separate components and gaps narrower than one voxel can be bridged. Use a voxel size of at most about half of the thinnest strut radius or wall thickness.

Parameters:

  • root – Node to analyze. It is prepared with the voxel size unless already prepared.

  • voxel_size – Positive sampling pitch in millimetres; the smallest component is used.

  • connectivity – 6 or 26.

  • bounds – Optional analysis box; it is intersected with the node’s bounding box.

  • root_already_prepared – Skip prepare() when the caller already prepared the node.

  • cull_far_cells – Decide whole 8^3 voxel blocks from one centre probe when the field there is farther from zero than 1.5 times the block’s half diagonal. Exact for true signed distances such as primitives, booleans and stochastic strut lattices (and any field with slope up to 1.5). TPMS fields can change faster than distance, so leave it off for designs that contain them.

Returns:

Component report.
